## The rule

Run the variant at **d4 and d8 only**, then check two gates against dense over the same range:

1. **eff mult at d8 > 1.0**
2. **the variant's own d4$\rightarrow$d8 exponent $\ge$ dense's, which is -0.0749**

If either fails, it loses at d12. Don't spend the run.

Gate 2 is the one that matters, because the exponent is what decides whether the advantage grows or decays. A shallower exponent than dense guarantees a crossing; you're only arguing about where.

## Cost and caveats

A d4+d8 variant pair at $\sim 1.3\times$ dense costs **21% of one dense d12 run**. So you can screen roughly five architectures for the price of one d12 confirmation.

Two things the rule won't do. It predicts the **sign** reliably but not the **magnitude**: the decay decelerates, so a two-point extrapolation over-predicts how bad d12 will be (it forecast 0.64$\times$ for D1, actual 0.72$\times$). And dense's own exponent isn't constant (-0.0749 over d4$\rightarrow$d8, -0.0572 over d8$\rightarrow$d12), so always compare a variant to dense over the same compute range, never to a number from a different range. That's the exact mistake I made earlier that produced the bogus 1.53$\times$.

For a new idea to be worth a d12 run, what you want to see at d8 is **eff > 1.0** with an exponent at or above **-0.0749**. Anything decaying between d4 and d8 is already answered.S
