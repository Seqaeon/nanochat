## Yes. The mechanism is the simplex, and it's measurable in the numbers you already have

The templates are additively mixed, and they're near-orthogonal. For K templates of comparable norm with mean pairwise cosine c:

```
||Σ αₖTₖ||_F / ||T||_F  =  sqrt( ||α||₂² + c(1 − ||α||₂²) )
uniform α:              =  sqrt( 1/K + (K−1)c/K )
```

At your measured cosines that's **0.40 to 0.55**. Uniform mixing produces an operator 1.8× to 2.5× smaller in Frobenius norm than any single template. So there's a large downhill gradient from uniform toward one-hot that has nothing to do with which template is right: concentrating is simply how you stop paying the averaging penalty. That's why the collapse is *total* (CV at the analytic max) rather than merely skewed.

The prediction that separates this from a generic "MoE routers collapse" story: the penalty scales with bank orthogonality, so **more orthogonal banks should collapse harder**. Your per-projection table:

## The fix: affine hull instead of convex hull

Keep the bank, drop the simplex. Let the coefficients be unconstrained and centered:

```
W_eff = T̄ + Σₖ βₖ(Tₖ − T̄),    T̄ = (1/K)ΣTₖ,   β = zero-init linear projection
```

which is algebraically just `αₖ = 1/K + (βₖ − β̄)`, so it's a **one-line replacement** for `return F.softmax(logits, dim=-1)` at `nanochat/gpt.py:2258` (`_template_weights`, plus the two inline copies in the compose paths):

```python
return (1.0 / K + logits - logits.mean(-1, keepdim=True)).to(dtype)
```

What that buys:

- **No norm incentive.** `T̄` is present at full weight always, so mixing no longer shrinks the operator. The force driving collapse is gone rather than penalized.
- **No starvation.** `∂L/∂βₖ = ⟨∂L/∂W_eff, Dₖ⟩`, full magnitude, independent per k, every step, forever. There's no probability mass to compete over so there's no fixed point.
- **α still sums to 1**, so it's still an affine combination and nothing about scale changes. What's dropped is only nonnegativity: the reachable set goes from the convex hull of the templates to their affine hull, and negative coefficients (subtracting a direction) become available for free.
- **Identity-preserving init that actually is one.** β = 0 gives `W_eff = T̄` exactly, versus the current situation where the intended init is partly overwritten anyway (the `basis.weight` orthogonal init bug from earlier).

Note what this is: base operator, plus independent unnormalized coefficients on a set of deviation directions. That is *literally the weight-space twin of ConditionedLinear's additive form*. The only difference is that the deviations here are full d×d matrices (O(d²) per DOF, crossing HBM) instead of rank-1 (O(d) per DOF, never materialized). Same fix, two spaces, and the cheap one is the layer I just built. For the paper that's a considerably stronger story than either finding alone: conditioning fails when the coefficients compete, and it fails the same way whether you condition weights or activations.

If you'd rather keep a normalized router, the middle option is spherical routing, `α = z/||z||₂`, giving `∂L/∂z_k = (g_k − α_k⟨α,g⟩)/||z||`. The leading term has no `α_k` factor, so starvation goes away, and the norm stays constant across routing decisions. Slightly more code and needs an epsilon guard at `z → 0`, so I'd default to the affine version.

## Novel ideas

I'll grade my own confidence, since that's the thing I got wrong before.

**1. The conditioning headroom spectrum.** *(genuinely novel as an application; the algebra is standard NTK)*

Every design in your repo asks "how do I make W depend on x". None asks the prior question: **is there anything at this layer for conditioning to exploit, and how much?**

There's an exact answer. The per-token weight gradient of a linear layer is rank-1: `g_t = a_t x_tᵀ` with `a_t = ∂L/∂y_t`. The mean `ḡ` is what the dense layer learns. **The dispersion of `g_t` around `ḡ` is precisely the capacity available to any input-conditioned scheme**, of any design. If tokens don't disagree about which way the weight should move, no conditional layer can beat dense, and no amount of K or R changes that.

That dispersion lives in `d²` dimensions, which sounds intractable, but you never need it. The `N×N` Gram of the per-token gradients factors exactly:

```
⟨g_s, g_t⟩_F = (a_s · a_t)(x_s · x_t)        so     G = (AAᵀ) ⊙ (XXᵀ)
```

A Hadamard product of two `N×N` inner-product matrices. Center it, take the eigenspectrum. You get, per layer, for free:

- **whether** conditioning can help: `λ_dispersion / ||ḡ||²`, the fraction of gradient signal a dense layer cannot capture
- **how much**: the participation ratio of that spectrum is the maximum useful K or R at that layer, computed before training anything
- **where**: run it per layer and you get the depth profile directly, which should predict your layers-1-to-6-are-dead result

This is one forward and backward pass on your existing d12 checkpoint plus two `4096×4096` eigendecompositions. It would tell you whether this entire research direction has headroom, and it's the measurement the program has been missing for 35 phases. The Gram factorization itself is textbook NTK algebra; using it as a per-layer conditioning-capacity diagnostic is what I haven't seen.

**2. Gradient-seeded specialization.** *(the constructive counterpart; adjacent work exists)*

The collapse is a symmetry-breaking failure, not a capacity failure: the router starts uninformative, so the first-order gradient (recover operator norm by concentrating) dominates the second-order one (find a useful partition, which requires templates to already be specialized). You cannot gradient-descend into a good partition from a uniform start.

So don't. Train dense, then initialize template `k` as `W_dense + ` the k-th eigendirection of the centered gradient dispersion above, and the router as the projection onto the corresponding token scores. Every template is better than dense on its own cluster *by construction*, and training refines instead of searching. MoEfication clusters neurons by co-activation; sparse upcycling copies weights and relies on router noise. Neither uses the per-token weight-gradient spectrum, which is the quantity that actually defines the right partition.

**3. Routing geometry as the paper.** *(the analysis result is real; I'd lead with it)*

"Convex mixing of near-orthogonal operators is norm-destructive and therefore self-collapsing" is a general statement about MoE weight-merging, model soups, SMEAR, LoRA mixtures, anything that averages operators on a simplex. ## The C NaN

Diagnosed. The recursion `q_i = c_i(p_i + Σ_{j<i} S_ij q_j)` with `S = VᵀU` is bounded at init only because `U = 0` makes `S = 0`. Nothing constrains `‖u‖` during training, and the substitution amplifies geometrically in m:

```
||u|| per gen   max|S|       m=4         m=8        m=16        m=32
        0.00     0.000   3.9e+00     4.3e+00     4.2e+00     4.0e+00
        2.00     0.227   5.2e+00     5.4e+00     5.1e+00     8.6e+00
        4.00     0.351   6.8e+00     1.0e+01     2.6e+01     5.6e+01
```

Muon gives fixed-magnitude orthogonalized updates regardless of gradient scale, so `mul_u` grows steadily with no counter-pressure. It's a question of when, not if. This is my bug: I identified the stability risk while designing it and then dropped the mitigation during a refactor.

## The C-arm fix

The composition was unbounded. `q_i = c_i(p_i + Σ_{j<i} S_ij q_j)` with `S = VᵀU` is bounded at init only because `U = 0` makes `S = 0`. ## What it now measures

Three numbers per layer, on top of the demand side you already had:

- **supply** — participation ratio of the per-token *Jacobian* dispersion, i.e. how many independent directions the layer's effective operator already varies along for free, because it's nonlinear
- **align** — centered kernel alignment between the demand Gram and the supply Gram, both over the same tokens. Dimension counts matching isn't enough; a layer could vary its operator a lot in directions nobody wants. This asks whether the free variation points where the gradients pull.

## What's fixed
