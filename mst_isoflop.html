MST · p12 isoFLOP profile

MST's optimum sits under dense's whole curve

Seven models trained to the same active training-FLOPs budget, each free to choose its own depth. This is the comparison that depends on no token-budget rule: fix the compute, let each architecture spend it however suits it, and read which one reaches the lower bits per byte. Every arm is plotted against six cost axes, and then against measured step time.

Reading it

01
L=24 is not broken. It is the right arm of the parabola. At a fixed compute budget, depth is bought with tokens. Past the optimum the token loss outweighs the capacity gain and bpb rises again. Dense does the same thing: L=14 (0.874479) is worse than L=12 (0.870475). MST L=24 sitting above MST L=16 is the profile working, not a defect in that configuration and not a consequence of the pinned head dimension.
02
MST wins on all six cost axes at its optimum, by very different margins. On FLOPs per token and on matrix parameters the win is clear. On total parameters it is marginal, because MST carries the streams it does not activate. That ordering is the paper's thesis stated as a picture: MST moves cost off the compute axis and onto the storage axis.
03
The FLOPs win does not yet convert into step time. At its optimum MST spends 908 ms per step against dense's 448, a factor of 2.03, while using 0.78× the FLOPs per token. That is the MFU gap showing up in the headline experiment. It does not weaken the isoFLOP result, which is a FLOPs claim and standard for an architecture paper, but a reviewer will ask and the paper should state it.
04
The margin is thin and rests on three points. One more MST arm fixes that. L=8 is the cheapest valid depth (sub_dim 128, two heads per stream) and lands on the left arm, where MST currently has a single point.
