What the numbers say

01
MST's advantage falls monotonically with depth, and crosses below parity by L=24. 1.51× at L=12, 1.06× at L=16, 0.83× at L=24 on active FLOPs per token. Same shape on active matrices: 2.19×, 1.26×, 0.88×. On total and active parameters MST is under 1.00× at every depth, which is expected and unchanged from the ladder.
02
That decay is collinear with starvation, not obviously with depth. Budget fraction runs 1.91×, 1.00×, 0.36× across the same three arms. MST L=24 is the most undertrained model in the experiment, more so than dense L=16 at 0.47×. A fixed token count set from MST L=16's own budget cannot rank arms whose optimal budgets span 5.3×.
03
One pair in this table is clean, and MST wins it. Dense L=12 sits at 1.01× its own budget and MST L=16 at 1.00×. Both are at their compute-optimal point, so neither is starved or overtrained. MST reads 1.06× on active FLOPs per token and 1.26× on active matrices against that pair. It is one pair, and the margin is thin.
04
Dense saturates inside this budget, which compresses the whole comparison. Dense L=14 to L=16 buys 0.0112 bpb for 1.41× the FLOPs. In a regime that flat, a cheaper-but-slightly-worse arm is punished hard on the implied-cost axis, because the dense curve is close to vertical. The L=12 multiplier is extrapolated past the measured dense range and should not be quoted.
Dense L=10 to L=16 and MST L=12/16/24, all SP2_k1 with per-stream value embeddings, dmodel = 64L rounded to a multiple of 128, N=4, T=2048, vocabulary 32,768. Multipliers use piecewise log-log interpolation on the four dense points; MST L=12 lies outside that range and is extrapolated.
